Why celebrate failures?

A theory that knows what it cannot do is more valuable than one that only knows what it can. Every NO-GO entry here represents a genuine attempt — a route that seemed promising, was pursued seriously, and was closed by a specific technical objection. The objections are real. They are on record. Red is the color of killed hypotheses — and of protected effort.

How to read a NO-GO

Each entry has four parts: the question (what was asked), the result (what actually happened), the failure mode (exactly where and why the chain broke), and the lesson (what this rules out and what it suggests instead). Do not re-attempt the approach without reading the source document. The celebration note explains why we're glad this failed.

The PF Methodology

The Propagation Framework derives its credibility not from claiming success at every turn, but from documenting failures as proudly as successes. This is primary evidence. A visitor to this museum should see that the framework has been tested, challenged, and has evolved through honest confrontation with its own limitations.
