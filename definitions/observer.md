## The Definition

**An observer is a stable mode or bound mode-structure that undergoes a distinguishable state change in response to an incoming propagation, producing a correlation between the observer's internal state and the propagating structure.**

An observer in this sense is a physical system. It is not necessarily conscious. It is not necessarily a person or an apparatus. It is any mode-structure that:

1. Is stable over the relevant timescale (so its state is meaningful before and after the interaction)
2. Interacts with an incoming propagation in a way that changes its own state
3. Produces a correlation between its post-interaction state and the properties of the incoming propagation

The observer is defined by the interaction, not by the nature of the system. Any system that meets these three conditions is an observer under this definition.

Plain language:

> An observer is anything that leaves a mark in response to something passing through the Medium.

## What Makes an Observer Different from a Passive Target?

A rock and a camera both absorb photons. A rock gets hotter; a camera records a pixel. What distinguishes them?

The distinction is not whether the interaction changes the observer's state — it does in both cases. The distinction is what kind of state change occurs and whether a recoverable correlation remains:

**Recording (camera):** The state change is localized and stable against overwrite. The pixel's new state is a record of the photon. The record is external to the photon's ongoing propagation.

**Absorption (rock):** The state change is thermalized. The photon's energy is dissipated as lattice vibrations. The recoverable correlation with the photon's phase, frequency, or arrival time is degraded to thermal noise. The correlation is not stably maintained.

**Re-emission (atom in excited state):** The state change (excitation) is followed by re-emission. The atom returns to its ground state and radiates a new photon. Some correlations may be preserved in the outgoing photon's properties (for example energy, angular momentum, timing, or phase in coherent scattering/stimulated-emission regimes). This is still a recording only to the extent that a specified correlation survives — the record travels.

All three involve state changes in response to propagation. The difference is whether the correlation survives in a form that can affect the observer's future behavior, propagate to another observer, or be queried.

## Taxonomy of Observation

The definition above encompasses a spectrum. Not all observers are equivalent.

| Type | Name | Correlation fate | Standard physics or PF? | Example |
|------|------|-----------------|------------------------|---------|
| 1 | Thermodynamic | Rapidly thermalized; lost | Standard physics | Rock absorbing sunlight |
| 2 | Structural recording | Maintained; locally queryable | Standard physics | CCD pixel, photographic plate |
| 3 | Correlated propagation | Maintained; propagates to another observer | Standard physics | Detector triggering readout, prepared atom re-emitting |
| 4 | Self-correlating | Feeds back into observer's own future response | PF interpretation — OPEN | Proposed: biological memory, neural encoding |

### Type 1 — Thermodynamic Observation

The observer's state change is thermalized and the correlation is rapidly degraded to environmental noise. The record is dissipated, not preserved in accessible form.

Examples: a rock absorbing sunlight; a solution absorbing a photon and re-radiating as heat; atmospheric molecules scattering light incoherently.

In these cases, the interaction meets the definition technically — there is a state change and a correlation — but the correlation is not maintained in a form that propagates further or influences subsequent behavior.

### Type 2 — Structural Recording

The observer's state change is localized, stable, and resistant to overwrite. The record can be queried and can influence subsequent interactions.

Examples: a photographic plate; a CCD pixel; a cavity mode storing a photon; a quantum dot; an atom prepared in a specific excited state.

The key property: the record persists and can be read. The correlation between observer state and propagating structure is maintained across the relevant timescale.

### Type 3 — Correlated Propagation

The observer's state change is re-emitted, propagated, or cascaded in a structured way. The correlation is preserved in the outgoing signal and can propagate to another observer.

Examples: a laser medium amplifying a photon; a neuron firing in response to a stimulus; a detector triggering a data acquisition system; a prepared atom re-emitting a photon with a specified surviving correlation (phase only in coherent regimes, not generic spontaneous fluorescence).

The key property: a specified correlation with the incoming propagation is preserved in the outgoing propagation. The observer is not a terminal sink but a relay.

### Type 4 — Self-Correlating Observation (Open — PF Interpretation)

The observer's state change affects the observer's own ongoing internal propagation in a way that changes the observer's future response to subsequent signals. The observer has internal state dynamics that are modified by the interaction.

Examples: (PF interpretation — no canonical claim) biological memory; a nervous system encoding experience; a mode whose coherence is altered by the interaction and where that alteration influences subsequent behavior.

**This is a PF interpretation.** The canonical definition does not require self-correlation. A Type 2 observer that simply records without internal back-action satisfies the definition. The claim that Type 4 observation occurs in biological systems — that consciousness involves self-modifying internal propagation — is consistent with the PF framework but is not derived from the canonical definitions and remains OPEN.

## The "Now" Problem

`time.md`'s canonical definition: "Time is the ordering and metric of state changes along physical histories." Proper time is the invariant duration measured along a timelike worldline; it is path-dependent.

For a timelike record-bearing observer, "now" is the current event on its worldline, labeled by its accumulated proper time. This is not a global time slice; it is local to the observer's worldline.

When an observer interacts with an incoming propagation:
- The interaction occurs at a specific event on the observer's worldline, labeled by proper time for timelike observers
- The state change is recorded at that point
- The observer's future state is conditioned on that record

The operational meaning of "now" for an observer: the current event on its worldline, together with the record of state changes up to that event. A Type 2 observer with no internal dynamics has a "now" that is just its current record and clock/worldline state. A Type 4 observer with self-correlating dynamics has a "now" conditioned on its full history.

Null excitations do not carry proper-time clocks. If a massless mode participates in an observation chain, the record-bearing observer is the timelike system that stores or relays the correlation (for example a detector, atom, or cavity apparatus), not a photon assigned its own proper-time "now."

**PF speculative connection:** `time.md`'s speculative section introduces the "local causal frontier" framing — the observer's past light cone as the boundary between causally determined and open regions. This is consistent with the proper-time account above and gives it a relativistic causal geometry. The local-causal-frontier picture is labeled speculative in `time.md` (observer-relative simultaneity cannot be a global canonical definition) and is not required for the present operational observer definition. It is noted here because several uses of "observer" in the PF framework implicitly rely on it.

## Observer and the Medium

`medium.md` says the Medium supports distinguishable change. An observer is a particular kind of distinguishable change: a stable mode that changes in response to another stable mode passing through.

The observer is not external to the Medium. The observer is a pattern in the Medium that responds to other patterns in the Medium. The observer-Medium relationship is not a subject-object split — it is a pattern-pattern interaction within the same rule-structure.

This matters for the measurement problem framing: "collapse" in quantum mechanics is sometimes described as the observer disturbing the system. In PF terms, there is no subject-object split — there is only mode-mode interaction. Whether a particular interaction is a "measurement" depends on whether the observer's state change produces a record that can propagate further. This is a structural question, not a question about consciousness or intention.

## What Observer Is NOT

- Not consciousness. The definition is operational and applies to a rock. The question of whether consciousness constitutes a distinct class of observer (Type 4) is an open research question, not a canonical claim.
- Not necessarily a classical system. The definition applies to quantum modes, atoms, cavity systems, detectors, and other quantum systems that meet the stability and correlation conditions. Do not assign a proper-time observer perspective to a bare null excitation.
- Not a passive camera. A camera records because its internal state is structured to be modifiable by signals in a specific way. The recording is an active state change, not a passive imprinting.
- Not a solution to the measurement problem. The definition does not determine whether "wave function collapse" is a physical process, an update of information, or something else. It provides a structural description of what an observation interaction does: it produces a record.
- Not a solution to the hard problem of consciousness. The PF does not have a canonical account of subjective experience. Type 4 observation is a PF interpretation that is consistent with some accounts of consciousness, but the framework does not claim to derive experience.
- Not a preferred reference frame. Observer in this definition is a local mode-structure. There is no global observer in the PF.

## Measurement Discipline

Every observer claim must specify:

1. **Observer type:** thermodynamic (Type 1), recording (Type 2), correlated propagation (Type 3), or self-correlating (Type 4 — PF interpretation only).
2. **Stability regime:** over what timescale is the record maintained? What is the mechanism of degradation or persistence?
3. **Correlation fidelity:** what property of the incoming propagation is correlated with the observer's state change? Phase, frequency, arrival time, polarization, position?
4. **Direction of record/correlation flow:** does the observer's record feed back into its own future propagation (Type 4), propagate to another observer (Type 3), or remain locally stored (Type 2)?
5. **Worldline/event anchoring:** at which event on the observer's worldline does the interaction occur? For timelike observers, what proper-time reading labels it? For null propagations, which timelike record-bearing system stores the correlation?
6. **Whether the claim is standard physics or PF interpretation:** the processes in Type 1–3 are standard physics (thermodynamic absorption, detectors, prepared systems); classifying them under this observer taxonomy is operational vocabulary. Type 4 self-correlation is PF interpretation.
7. **Medium role:** the observer is a pattern in the Medium; the observer-Medium boundary is a distinction of convenience, not a metaphysical split.

## Falsification Conditions

An observer definition fails if:

1. **No distinguishable state change occurs:** if the observer's state is unchanged by the interaction, it is not an observer under this definition.
2. **No correlation with the incoming propagation:** if the observer's state change carries no recoverable correlation with the properties of the incoming propagation, the interaction is not an observation.
3. **The record cannot be stable on the relevant timescale:** if the state change is immediately thermalized before any record can be read, the interaction is Type 1, not Type 2 or 3.
4. **A Type 4 observer claim has no proposed mechanism:** if the claim is that an observer's internal propagation is self-modifying, the mechanism by which the record back-acts on the observer's coherence must be specified. Without a mechanism, it is not a canonical PF derivation.
5. **PF self-correlation interpretation is inconsistent with decoherence theory:** if Type 4 observation is claimed but environmental decoherence degrades the self-correlation before it can affect subsequent behavior, the claim requires revision.
