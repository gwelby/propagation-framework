## Axiom 1: Propagation Is Fundamental

**Statement:** The most basic thing that exists is propagation — the movement of distinguishable change through a Medium.

Propagation is prior to:
- **Time** — which is how propagation registers to an embedded observer
- **Energy** — which is the frequency of a propagation mode
- **Matter** — which is a stable self-reinforcing propagation pattern
- **Force** — which is the bending of propagation in a Medium with varying properties

The Medium is not a substance. It is the rule-structure that makes propagation possible. This is deliberately analogous to Euclid beginning with points and lines without defining what they are made of. We begin with propagation without defining what propagates through what. The theory describes the structure of propagation.

**Canonical reference:** `medium.md` defines the Medium by its operational roles, not as a substance. `propagation.md` formalizes propagation as a primitive.

**What this axiom does:**
- Establishes propagation as the primitive starting point of the PF
- Makes the Medium a role-based concept rather than a substance claim
- Rules out non-propagating substrates as physically meaningful foundations

**What this axiom does not do:**
- Define what propagates (the content is in the mode structure)
- Claim the Medium is made of anything specific
- Derive specific propagation speeds or field equations

## Axiom 2: Every Medium Has a Causal Velocity

**Statement:** In any medium through which propagation occurs, there exists a maximum finite speed at which controllable causal influence can travel. We call this the causal velocity of that medium.

In vacuum, the causal velocity is *c* = 299,792,458 m/s. In other media, excitation/propagation speeds may be lower than *c*, but the front-velocity constraint — the maximum speed at which a discontinuity in the field can propagate — remains bounded by *c* in all physical media. Causal velocity is always finite and measurable. It is always the boundary that separates what can influence what from what cannot.

The causal velocity determines:
- **Causal structure** — what can communicate with what
- **Maximum controllable signal speed** — the front-velocity bound for information-bearing influence
- **Dispersion constraints** — how frequency, wavelength, and allowed mode velocities are related in a specified medium
- **Threshold behavior in some systems** — for example Cherenkov-like phase-velocity thresholds in effective media; other phase transitions require separate criticality models

**Canonical reference:** `causal_velocity.md` defines causal velocity precisely as the maximum speed of controllable causal influence, derived from the Medium's graph structure.

**What this axiom does:**
- Makes locality meaningful — causal velocity is the boundary of what can influence what
- Sets the speed limit for all propagation
- Enables the distinction between Type 1 (thermodynamic) and Type 2+ (recording) observers via causal cones

**What this axiom does not do:**
- Derive the specific value of c in vacuum (that's a measurement)
- Imply relativistic invariance — the causal velocity of a given medium may not be Lorentz invariant
- Resolve whether quantum entanglement involves a medium with a causal velocity

## Axiom 3: Coherence Is the Necessary Condition for Structure

**Statement:** Propagation without stable relational organization disperses into incoherent disturbance. Structure arises when propagation satisfies the relevant coherence or stability condition for its domain: stable phase relationships for waves, off-diagonal density-matrix structure for quantum states, persistent organized correlations for dynamical systems, or invariant mode structure for fields.

Coherence exists on a spectrum:

| Level | Description | Physical example |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| Zero coherence | Thermal noise, maximum entropy | Blackbody radiation |
| Partial coherence | Statistical regularities, transient structures | Partial lasers, weather patterns |
| High coherence | Stable patterns, persistent structures | Atoms, molecules, lasers |
| Self-referential coherence | Systems whose organized dynamics include models of their own state/process | Biological memory and consciousness candidates — OPEN |

Every stable structure in the framework must satisfy some domain-specific coherence or stability condition above the relevant background. The degree and kind of coherence determines the degree and kind of structure.

**Canonical reference:** `coherence.md` defines the four-layer coherence structure (noise → structural → functional → self-referential). This axiom corresponds to layer 1 of `coherence.md` as the necessary condition for all higher layers.

**What this axiom does:**
- Explains why structure exists at all (maximum entropy alone would be uniform noise)
- Sets the foundation for stable modes; quantization requires the domain-specific mode structure in `mode.md`
- Explains the distinction between energy (coherence-free propagation) and matter (coherent mode structures)
- Provides the bootstrap for why the universe has anything worth modeling

**What this axiom does not do:**
- Explain why specific coherence patterns are stable (that's the Minimal Winding Principle)
- Derive the specific coherence lifetimes of specific modes
- Explain consciousness or self-referential coherence in full — that requires the full Type 4 observer structure

## Axiom 3b: Minimal Winding Principle (Corollary)

**Statement:** Among coherent states in the same topological class, the stable fundamental PF mode is the one with minimal topological winding.

Among all possible coherent configurations, the propagation medium selects the simplest one. The mode with winding number k = 1 is fundamental; modes with k > 1 are excited or composite states. A mode with higher winding requires more phase relationships to be maintained simultaneously, making it less stable under perturbation.

**Application:** For helical modes with resonance ratio k = J_z/J_θ, Axiom 3b selects k = 1 (the primitive loop). In the current PF claim matrix, this principle is used in the Weinberg-angle derivation via the Casimir polynomial `x² + C₂x − C₂ = 0`, whose positive root gives the reported PF value `sin²θ_W ≈ 0.22310`. This definition file records the selection principle only; numerical claim status, confidence, and remaining bridge conditions belong in `CLAIMS.md` and the relevant derivation audits, not in this root definition.

**Canonical reference:** This corollary is used in `mode.md` for particle mode selection. Whether Axiom 3b follows from Axiom 3 alone remains open; for the current framework it is an explicitly named corollary/selection principle.

**What this corollary does:**
- Resolves the selection problem that Axiom 3 alone does not address
- Provides a mode selection principle without aesthetic preferences

## Falsification Conditions

1. **Axiom 1 fails if:** A physically meaningful system exists that does not propagate — something fundamentally at rest without prior propagation, not merely something with a stable mode. A true non-propagating substrate would contradict Axiom 1.

2. **Axiom 2 fails if:** Controllable FTL signaling is demonstrated in any medium. The causal velocity is the boundary of controllable causal influence; if controllable influence exceeds it in any medium, Axiom 2 is falsified.

3. **Axiom 3 fails if:** A stable coherent mode structure forms in a domain where the relevant coherence condition for that domain is demonstrably unsatisfied — e.g., a stable atom exists without any quantum coherence condition being satisfied, or a stable molecular configuration forms without any binding/correlational coherence. This is domain-specific, not a claim about all structure arising from no conditions whatsoever.

4. **Axiom 3b fails if:** The propagation medium consistently selects higher-winding modes as fundamental rather than k = 1 modes, without energetic justification. The Minimal Winding Principle would need revision.
